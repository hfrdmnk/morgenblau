## morgen.blue landing page

From `site/src/pages/index.astro`, as shipped. He wrote every section; typo fixes are the only edits, and the one sentence an agent rewrote is left out.

The daily web newspaper. Curated by you.

Morgenblau gathers your favorite blogs, YouTube channels and newsletters into finite daily digests. So you never miss the content you actually care about. And when you’re done, you’re done.

It often seems like the authentic web is dead. We get sucked into rabbit holes, algorithms show us content we don’t actually care about and we spend way more time on infinite feeds than we intended to. It doesn’t have to be this way. The content you care about is still out there.

**We just have the wrong tools**

Social feeds. An algorithm assembles an infinite feed for you. With one goal: Making you spend as much time as possible on the platform. You are never done. And the posts from creators you actually want to see might not even reach you. The algorithm decides.

Newsletters. The best content often gets sent via email. But it might get buried in your inbox or not even reach it (hello spam folder). Newsletters deserve to be read in a calm environment. And your inbox deserves to not get filled with even more unread messages.

Feed readers. RSS readers are the best tool we got for taking back control over our feeds. But they still have one problem: They can quickly turn into a chore because they tell you exactly how many posts you haven’t read yet. That shouldn’t be the case.

**A finite feed.**

Curate your sources. Add blogs, newsletters, YouTube channels and more. Morgenblau also makes it easy to import subscriptions from other tools.

Use Morgenblau as your daily web newspaper. All the content that was published today is presented in one calm list. Morgenblau doesn’t artificially boost or hide posts. It only prioritizes sources that you explicitly marked as important to you.

Go on with your day. When you reach the end of the list, you’re done. Previous days are still available to read if you want to, but that’s your choice. One that you can explicitly make. Or decide not to. It’s up to you.

**All the content you don’t want to miss. In one feed.**

Newsletters, out of your inbox. You get a private email address. Use it to sign up to your favorite newsletters. From now on, they arrive in your curated feed, not in your inbox between bills and work mail.

Channels without the recommendations. Don’t miss a video from the channels you follow. And then watch them in a calm interface without any distracting sidebars or recommendations. You can even filter out Shorts.

A quiet place to read. Morgenblau’s interface is intentionally calm and simple. So the only thing that takes your attention is the content. Set in beautiful typography and, depending on your device, in light or dark mode.

Save the gems. If you want to keep a post for later, simply save it to your personal library. Use tags to organize and find them later.

Follow (almost) every site. Even if your favorite blog doesn’t have an RSS feed, Morgenblau can still let you follow it. You shouldn’t have to manually visit websites every day, just because they don’t have a feed. Morgenblau can do that for you.

**For those of you that still miss Google Reader**

At its core, Morgenblau is a feed reader. Import your subscriptions from any RSS app with OPML, export them whenever you like, and follow anything that has a feed. Social features are coming, for those that want them.

**Your data belongs to you.**

You sign in with your Atmosphere account, the same one you might use on platforms like Bluesky. Your feed subscriptions and saves live in your own ATProto account as public records, not locked inside Morgenblau. You can use them with other apps, build your own or simply take your data wherever you want to go. Private data is coming soon as well.

**Frequently asked questions**

What is Morgenblau? A reader for the web you curate. You follow blogs, newsletters, YouTube channels and websites, and Morgenblau gathers what they publish into one web newspaper per day.

Can I use it today? Morgenblau is in an invite-only alpha while I finish the essentials. Subscribe to the newsletter and you’ll hear when it opens up.

Do I need to know what RSS is? No. Paste the address of a website or a YouTube channel and Morgenblau works out the rest. For newsletters, you sign up with your Morgenblau address.

What happens to the days I skip? Nothing. They don’t pile up and no unread count makes you feel guilty. Every past daily digest stays available if you want to go back.

Can I take my subscriptions with me? Yes. Import and export your feeds as OPML, the format every feed reader understands. Your feed subscriptions also live in your own ATProto account.

**Why I’m creating (not) another RSS reader.**

Hi, I’m Dominik.

Developer, Student, Runner and a big fan of the open web. I write my own blog and love to read others’. Which made me a big RSS aficionado over the years.

As a digital native, RSS readers always felt like this almost ancient but very calm technology to me. One that I’m still surprised is still around and so widely used.

Unfortunately, most feed readers also feel a bit ancient at times and not much has changed since Google Reader was shut down. They almost all have the same layout and, my biggest gripe, use a system where posts are treated as read or unread.

I wanted to change that and build my own ideal minimal reader. Along the way, I’m exploring how newer technologies like the AT Protocol can enable an even better experience for users.

The old web, the interesting bloggers, the small authentic creators: they are still out there and creating original content every day. You just have to rediscover them.

Morgenblau helps you with that.

Three side-effects of this system that are a feature, not a bug:

- If I save many articles on a day, they clog up my todo list. This makes me more conscious of what I want to save in the first place.
- If I don’t manage to read an article on any given day, I have to move it to the next day. This creates friction. If I do this a couple of days in a row, it starts to annoy me. A good sign that I can delete the article from my list altogether.
- If I really do want to read an article, I make time for it because it’s now on my todo list. With the same priority as, let’s say, doing laundry. Not something to do “sometime”.

But what about saving articles for later so I can revisit them?

I don’t think there is a point in doing that anymore. If an article truly resonates with me, I should probably take the time and write a post about it. Like I did yesterday. This makes me reflect on the article and I won’t forget it so easily.

For other articles that I find shareable, I post them over on Bluesky and they get backfilled onto my personal website, so I have my little archive there.

You see, you don’t need much for a good read-later system. Not even a new app.

## My simpler home on the internet

From https://dominikhofer.me/simpler-internet-home/ (August 16, 2026).

Back in January, I started an experiment by turning this website into a social media timeline-like experience. But now, we’re back to the roots.

I like how each iteration of my personal website tells a different story. It’s a snapshot of how I wanted to represent myself online at the time of creating it.

With the first version, I mainly wanted a place where people could find me, learn more about me, get into writing regularly, and where I can showcase my work and photography.

Afterwards, I dove more and more into the IndieWeb and started thinking about how a personal website can replace social media profiles while still feeling up-to-date and casual. That was the next iteration, version two.

But when looking at this second version, I felt like there was a disconnect between the vision of the website and who I am. The website felt cramped, there was too much going on, quick link share posts mixed with long-form content. It was a cool concept, a bit of everything, but not what my home on the internet should be.

What I want people to feel when they visit my website is that it’s a space to breathe and a pause from the busy rest of the internet. A place to explore on their own terms and find interesting things.

That’s why my writing is now at the core of this website. Right on the home page, you see a curation of my personal favorites to get you started (as a new visitor) or the latest posts (if you’re returning). The writing is the living part of this website and what makes it unique. The website itself should be the stage and not the star of the show.

The theme of “simplification” runs deep within this website now. From the h1 on the home page to the technical choices to the design.

While I love the ethos of Kirby CMS, I felt like it was overkill for a website of my scale. I don’t need a CMS. I simply need a folder with markdown files in it that powers the whole thing.

What I (or rather you as a reader) also don’t need are five different RSS feeds. Now, we just have one with only my long-form post. Not a cluttered one with everything mixed in.

No short-form social media-like posts either. They encourage posting quick thoughts that sound smart but aren’t, if you sit longer with them. Writing is thinking on paper, and that can’t happen in 200-ish characters.

The photos section is gone as well. I still love taking photos (mainly on vacation), but I never regularly uploaded them to the website anyways. I might add a static section to the site someday though, where I showcase my favorite photos I took over the last few years.

All of this sounds like I only removed stuff from the site. But simplification doesn’t always have to equal sizing down. It means making things simpler.

For me. Updating my reading list or sharing interesting links (both on /now) is a breeze now, since I don’t even have to update my website for that. It happens automatically, via ATProto services I use anyways: Bookhive for tracking the books I read and Semble for collecting links.

And for you. You can now not only follow this website via the RSS feed. There is also an option to get notified via email whenever I publish a new post. Or, if you’re into ATProto and know what it is, you can follow and read the blog via Standard.site. See both options at the bottom of this post (and every other page).

I hope with this redesign, my website returns to what I originally wanted out of it: A personal home on the internet that feels truly personal.
