This article has been carefully prepared to test what happens when readability and the sanitizer meet hostile markup. The article body itself is meaningful prose so that readability accepts it as content, but the markup contains things that should never reach the rendered page.

For example, an event handler attached to a paragraph: click me. The sanitizer must strip the inline handlers without touching the surrounding text.

Equally, a script tag embedded inside the body: . The sanitizer must drop the script element entirely.

Inline styles such as this colour are typically allowed, but anything resembling JavaScript-flavoured CSS — for example a sneaky background — must be removed.

The text content must survive the cleaning, otherwise readability will reject the article entirely. Several more paragraphs follow to ensure there is enough volume for readability to bite.

The slow afternoon spread itself across the room like a cat across a windowsill. The clock ticked but did not press. Outside, the bell of a slow bicycle passed and faded, and somewhere on the street a dog barked, briefly, and was quiet.

She turned the page. The book did not ask her to hurry. The book did not, in fact, ask her anything. That was the point of the book. It sat there, patient, content, and indifferent to whether she read it now or in an hour or never at all.

There was a particular pleasure in that indifference. The page did not light up when you looked at it. The page did not record that you had visited. The page did not, late in the evening, send a notification reminding you that you had not read it today.

The book waited. She read. The day continued.
