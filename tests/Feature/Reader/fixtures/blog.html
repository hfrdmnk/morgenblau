On Reading Slowly

Published Tuesday, by Grace Hopper

There is a habit of reading that I lost in the years between 2010 and 2020. I had not noticed it leaving, only that one morning I sat down with a book and discovered I could no longer hold a paragraph in my head. The sentences began but my mind did not. I had become a feed.

I do not blame the platforms — that is too easy, and also too true to be useful. The platforms responded to a demand. The demand was for a kind of reading that did not commit, that could be interrupted, that did not ask the reader to remain. I had wanted such reading. I had asked for it daily, by clicking.

The recovery has been slower than the loss. It started with re-reading old books, books whose plots I already knew, books that did not require me to remember what came before. Childhood books. Comfort books. A reading without stakes, almost a reading without reading.

Then longer books, books I had never read but had always meant to. I read them in the morning, before the day began to demand things. I read them on paper, because the screen carried with it the habits of the screen — the impulse to swipe, the eye that drifted toward notifications, the implicit knowledge that something more interesting was always one tap away.

What surprised me most, in this slow return, was how much of the pleasure of reading turned out to be a pleasure of attention itself. The plot mattered less than I had supposed. The prose mattered more. The experience of being inside a sentence, of inhabiting the writer's mind for the length of an idea, was the thing I had been missing.

I am still not the reader I once was. But I am closer to her than I was a year ago, and closer still than the year before that. The recovery is a discipline, but it is also, increasingly, a relief. I no longer feel guilty for being slow. I feel suspicious of being fast.

If you are reading this and it has taken you longer than you expected — longer than the headline promised, longer than your habit allows — that is not a failure of the writing. That is the writing working. Slow down. Read the next sentence. Then the one after that.

The book is patient. The book will wait.
