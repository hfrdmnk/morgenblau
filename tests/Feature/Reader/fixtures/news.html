Local Library Reports Surge in Slow-Reading Memberships

By Margaret Hamilton · Culture

The Old Town Public Library reported a 38 percent increase in the membership of its "slow reading" programme over the past calendar year, according to figures released by the library's communications office on Monday.

The programme, founded in 2022, asks participants to commit to reading a single physical book for one hour per week without checking any device. Members report their progress on paper, in a logbook kept at the front desk, and meet monthly for an unstructured discussion.

"We did not expect this to scale," said the programme's coordinator, who declined to be named for fear of attracting press attention. "The whole point of it is that it does not scale. You either come in and sit down and read, or you do not."

According to the library's data, the average age of slow-reading members has dropped by nearly a decade since the programme began. In 2022, the median member was 58. As of last week, the median member is 41.

The shift is visible in the books being borrowed. Whereas the early programme leaned heavily on what one might call comfort literature — long Victorian novels, Russian classics, the canonical lyric poets — newer members are checking out contemporary essays, short novels in translation, and what one librarian described as "the kind of books you would normally read in the queue at a coffee shop, but slowly."

Asked whether the increase represents a broader cultural shift or a local quirk, the coordinator was non-committal. "I do not know what is happening in other libraries. I know that we have more people here than we used to. They sit. They read. They leave. That is the whole programme."

The library has no plans to expand the programme, automate the logbook, or accept digital reading time as a substitute for the in-person hours. "The point is the hour in the chair," the coordinator said. "If we let people count the time they spent reading on their phone at the bus stop, we are running a different programme."

For now, the waiting list to join is two weeks long.
